A photographic field guide to the deer family: the fifty odd species of cervids living across forests, tundra, wetlands and mountain slopes, and the people working to keep them there.

Every photograph was made in the field, at a distance, without bait or calls.

Meet the deer family

Cervids are hoofed mammals defined by one habit: most males grow and shed a set of antlers every year. Four of the best known members:

  • Red deer

    Cervus elaphus. The largest deer in Europe and the classic rutting stag of British parks. Bulls bellow at dawn in October.

  • White-tailed deer

    Odocoileus virginianus. The most widespread large mammal in North America, recognisable by the white tail it flashes as an alarm signal.

  • Mule deer

    Odocoileus hemionus. A deer of the American West named for its large ears. It bounds with a stiff legged leap rather than running.

  • Axis deer

    Axis axis. A spotted deer of the Indian subcontinent, critically dependent on open grassland.

Wildlife photography, the quiet way

Four rules we follow

  • Keep your distance. A 300mm lens and patience beat a closer approach.
  • Watch the animal, not the light. If a deer stops feeding to stare at you, you are too close.
  • No bait, no calls, no playback during rut season, because males are defensive and energy is scarce.
  • Leave no trace: stay on marked tracks and pack out everything you carried in.
